The reward an agent can afford, against the one it stands in for. Every one of the 105 unrooted topologies on this leaf set, scored twice: at fixed known parameters, and at parameters maximized per topology by the same optimizer the Potts and hidden Markov instances use. Fixture: 4-state Jukes-Cantor, 1500 sites, seed 20260905; the fixed branch length is the generating tree's mean, 0.1644. (a) The two surfaces, correlation 0.9568; both score the generating topology highest, so the cheap reward and the expensive one agree on the answer while disagreeing in detail. The correlation is linear rather than rank-based because the fitted surface does not totally order topologies: several of its optima agree to within the optimizer's own convergence, so their relative order is not a property of the model. (b) The cheap surface has one free parameter, and the conclusion does not rest on it: across a 50-fold range of fixed branch length the two surfaces pick the same best topology in 7 of 7 cases, with the correlation never falling below 0.8719. The substitution is what makes training affordable at all: the fitted reward runs a full optimization per candidate, the known one a single pruning pass.
