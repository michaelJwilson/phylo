The reward an agent can afford, against the one it stands in for. Every one of the 105 unrooted topologies on this leaf set, scored twice: at fixed known parameters, and at parameters maximized per topology by the same optimizer the Potts and hidden Markov instances use. Fixture: 4-state Jukes-Cantor, 1500 sites, seed 20260905; the fixed branch length is the generating tree's mean, 0.1644. (a) The two surfaces, rank correlation 0.9045; both rank the generating topology first, so the cheap reward and the expensive one agree on the answer while disagreeing in detail. (b) The cheap surface has one free parameter, and the conclusion does not rest on it: across a 50-fold range of fixed branch length the two surfaces pick the same best topology in 7 of 7 cases, with the rank correlation never falling below 0.7775. The substitution is what makes training affordable at all: the fitted reward runs a full optimization per candidate, the known one a single pruning pass.
